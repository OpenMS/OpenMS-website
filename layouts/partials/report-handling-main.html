Report handling manual

How the OpenMS Code of Conduct Committee follows up on reports — so responses stay consistent, fair, and focused on community health.

Report an incident Code of Conduct

Values

How we decide

Enforcing the Code of Conduct is not taken lightly. These guidelines keep the Committee consistent and fair.

01

Stay personal

Engage the parties to understand the situation, while respecting privacy. The goal is a healthier community, not only a formal decision.

02

Empathy over labels

Avoid binary “good” and “bad”. Clear aggression is addressed firmly; harder cases need context and a path that re-engages everyone.

03

Respect over email

Criticism by email can be isolating. Keep open-minded respect, stay transparent, and treat every member fairly.

04

Watch for bias

Discrimination can be subtle or unconscious. If you were treated unfairly, say so — these procedures exist so the complaint is heard.

05

Better discussion

Identify where conversation broke down, and offer actionable pointers that can lead to positive change.

06

Support newcomers

Give new members explicit support, with the aim of increasing participation from underrepresented groups.

07

Language and culture

Honest misunderstandings happen across languages. Help people see the issue and what to change, without shutting them out.

Process

Mediation

Voluntary informal mediation is available when it can help — for example when several parties have escalated. No one can be pressured to take part.

  1. Find a mediator

    The Committee proposes a candidate who can serve as mediator.

  2. Reporter agrees

    Reporters may decline mediation or propose an alternate mediator.

  3. Reported person agrees

    The reported person(s) must also agree before the process moves forward.

  4. Settle terms

    Parties may propose a different mediator. All terms need common agreement.

  5. Set a timeline

    Aim to complete mediation within two weeks. The mediator then reports to the Committee.

Urgent

Clear and severe breaches

Personal threats and violent, sexist, or racist language are handled immediately, in addition to the regular report process.

  1. Disconnect

    Immediately disconnect the originator from all OpenMS communication channels.

  2. Confirm to the reporter

    Reply that the report was received and that the originator has been disconnected.

  3. Explain to the originator

    Contact them with how the language or actions qualify, and that they may ask the Committee for a review.

  4. Committee sign-off

    Every use of this mechanism is formally reviewed so it is not used to shut down ordinary disagreement.

Process

How reports are handled

Receipt is confirmed within 72 hours — sooner when possible. The Committee may gather more information before acting, including on behalf of the Executive Committee.

  1. What happened

    Establish a written account of the incident from the available evidence.

  2. Was it a violation

    Decide whether the event constitutes a Code of Conduct violation.

  3. Who is responsible

    Identify the responsible party or parties.

  4. Is anyone at risk

    Check whether the situation is ongoing or a threat to anyone’s physical safety.

Records

Written archive

Deliberations are recorded on a private mailing list. Off-list calls are summarized back to the list. The Committee aims to agree a resolution within two weeks, or send an update with a projected timeline.

Outcomes

Resolutions

The Committee agrees by consensus. If it deadlocks for over a week, the matter goes to the Executive Committee. Before a resolution is enacted, the original reporter and other affected parties are asked whether it is acceptable.

  1. No further action

    If no violation occurred, or the matter was resolved publicly while the Committee was considering it.

  2. Mediation

    If all involved parties agree, the Committee may facilitate voluntary mediation.

  3. Public reminder

    Point out behaviour judged inappropriate in context, and ask the community to self-adjust.

  4. Private reprimand

    The group chair emails the individual(s), copying the Committee.

  5. Public reprimand

    Delivered in the same venue as the violation where practical, and may be published for the record.

  6. Apology

    A public or private apology may be requested if the reporter agrees. The reporter may refuse further contact.

  7. Hiatus

    A mutually agreed break, or a mandatory cooling-off period if the person will not step back voluntarily.

  8. Ban

    A temporary or permanent ban from some or all OpenMS spaces. Bans are recorded for later review.

Integrity

Conflicts of interest

If a Committee member has a conflict of interest, they must notify the others immediately and recuse themselves if necessary. The Committee never discusses cases in public; public statements are made by the Committee chair or the Executive Committee.
